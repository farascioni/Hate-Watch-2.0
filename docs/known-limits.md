## Known limits

**In short:** Everything rests on ESPN's unofficial, undocumented API, so its delay is our floor and a change to its fields can break a league until the code catches up; the replays are the early warning. Some alerts are inferred rather than read (the NHL goalie in net, MLB runs charged to whoever is pitching), some leagues lack data ESPN doesn't publish (soccer injuries and streaks, F1 injuries), and some paths have only been tested on simulations or recordings. Each limit below says why it exists and what to do about it, where there's anything.

- **ESPN's API is undocumented and unofficial.** Field names, shapes and wording can change without notice, and nothing promises it stays up. *Why:* there's no official free feed for these leagues. *Workaround:* every test uses ESPN's real shapes and wording, and `node test/replay.ts` (finished games, tracked score against the final) is the canary; run it after anything looks off. The images and clips that lean on ESPN's rights can be switched off on the server without a build (`server/src/flags.ts`, CLAUDE.md "Release flow").
- **ESPN's delay is the floor.** A play reaches us when ESPN posts it, tens of seconds after it happens (see "Real-time"). *Workaround:* a lower-latency feed per league through the same `NPlay` interface, listed at the end of "Real-time".
- **Hockey waits for ESPN to finish a play** (a shot 20 seconds, an unnamed penalty up to 10 minutes, a goal with no scorer 90 seconds, a repeat of a shot a minute; a shot ESPN posts more than 3 minutes late alerts nobody), so those alerts come that much later, and a pulled goalie's shots faced can miss the last 20 seconds of saves. A hockey game that goes to a shootout finishes only when the scoreboard says Final (no fallback if it never does). docs/alerts.md, "Revisions: ESPN changes a hockey play after its alert".
- **The NHL goalie in net is inferred.** ESPN doesn't say who was in goal for each goal, so the tracker seeds each team's goalie from the box score when it first reads a game (`seedGoalies` in `live.ts`), then moves it to whoever makes each save, and to a goalie coming on as a substitute (`observePlay` in `detectors.ts`), but never on a play with no team and never to a goalie the box score puts on the other side (`goalieTeam`). ESPN's NHL feed has no goalie-change or empty-net play (0 of 314 in EDM at LA, October 13, 2026): a backup coming on after an injury reads as a pull, and the shootout, overtime, fights and ejections are untested on live data. An empty-net goal is nobody's: it gets no goalie alert, and the team that gave it up gets `nhl.team.empty_net_goal` ("… gave up an empty-netter to the …") instead.
- **MLB "gives up runs" is charged to the pitcher who put the runner on, but earned runs are not told apart.** Each scoring runner's run goes to the pitcher he reached against (`chargedRuns`, `g.owner`), so a reliever's inherited runner is the starter's run, as in the box score. When the scorers can't be told from ESPN's text, or don't add up to the play's runs, the pitcher on the play gets them all. A result that is still "Official Scorer Ruling Pending" after 45 seconds is alerted without that sentence, and if the ruling then makes it an error, no error alert follows (a ruling that comes in time is judged). Earned or not is ESPN's later ruling: a "no quality start" alert quotes the earned runs as of its moment (NLCS Game 2: 3 ER, lowered to 2 ten minutes later). *Workaround for what's official:* blown saves, no quality start and the loss are read from ESPN's box score (`boxPitchers`, `pitcherEvents`), which uses the official numbers.
- **Standings and injury alerts fire on change, and the first read is a silent baseline.** The last read is kept per league in the server's `kv` table (`standings:<league>`, `injuries:<league>`; `scanStandings`, `scanInjuries` in `live.ts`). A fresh database, a new server, or a newly added league starts with a baseline, so a change that happened before that first read never alerts. College football has no injury scan.
- **A football score taken back keeps its title.** ESPN taking a touchdown back puts "ESPN has since taken this score back." on the alert, but the title still says the team scored (Kennesaw State's phantom touchdown, KENN at MOST); a phantom is mostly held now (`SCORE_WORDS_WAIT_MS`), so this is for what ESPN takes back after the hold. Penalty flags are most college football feed rows (21 of 41 in KENN at MOST); that is by design.
- **A restart forgets what the server only keeps in memory.** Football revisions of plays posted before a restart, late lines for alerts sent before it, and MLB holds (an ABS challenge, a half-inning's end) are lost; the database's ids still stop anything going out twice. The full list is in [docs/duplicate-alerts.md](duplicate-alerts.md), "What survives a restart". *Workaround:* deploy when nothing is live.
- **F1's live path has only been tested on simulations and one recorded weekend.** In-race DNFs and double DNFs are tested with a simulated race through the real engine (`test/f1-live.test.ts`), and the timing (the running order's gaps, pit stops, lapped and places lost) the same way (`test/f1-timing.test.ts`). The timing was read from ESPN during the 2026 Singapore GP's last laps; ESPN only said "GREEN", then "CHECKER" at the end, so any other flag shows as ESPN words it, made readable ("SAFETY_CAR" is "Safety car"; `flagLabel` in `f1.ts`).
- **F1 sessions with alerts are qualifying, the sprint and the race** (`sessionKind` in `f1.ts`). Sprint qualifying (ESPN's "SS", the sprint shootout) and practice send none. F1 has no injury report on ESPN, so no F1 injury alerts.
- **Soccer has no injury alerts, no losing streaks and no elimination.** ESPN's EPL injury report is empty, the table carries no streak, and a league season has no playoff to be knocked out of (a drop into the relegation zone is its alert instead). "Loses the ball" and "Gives the ball away" are judged from the touch after (`soccerTouch`), since ESPN doesn't mark a pass or dribble as failed. A recording of Manchester City at Liverpool (October 11, 2026) is on the Mac for replaying them (`replay-epl.ts`); ESPN's retyping of touches and the pairs it moves are judged again (docs/alerts.md, "Soccer passes and dribbles").
- **Clips exist only where ESPN has them:** MLB, the NBA, the WNBA and the NHL (`CLIP_LEAGUES` in `live.ts`; ESPN had none for the NFL or soccer in October 2026), plus a lost UFC fight's clip.
- **Favorite teams trust the device's word that it has Plus, and forget their games on a restart.** Plus is the store's answer on the device (`expo-iap`), so the server stores a favorite from any device that sends one; the game registry that tells it who a favorite is playing is in memory and fills again as each tracker attaches (`docs/favorites.md`). Only the opponent team's own alerts route, not its players'. *Workaround:* none needed for a small app; the server would need a receipt check to enforce Plus.
- **The lock screen scoreboard is untried on a device, and the app starts it.** The server pushes its updates, but only the app can start it (no push-to-start), so it returns when the app opens; the lock screen's height limit and Apple's update budget are unchecked. F1 and the UFC are not on it (`docs/live-activity.md`).
- **Recent misery is as complete as what the server reads.** It adds no ESPN call: it records losses from the scoreboard every league already reads, and everything else from the alerts the server already makes. F1's results are read only while someone tracks an F1 driver or constructor, and the UFC's fights only when a tracked fighter is in them, so an untracked fighter's loss in an untracked fight has no row (docs/recent-misery.md, "What isn't covered").
- **The server is one machine on one volume.** A lost volume, a failed host or a Fly outage in `iad` takes it down until someone acts. Since October 13, 2026 a lost volume loses about a second of data, not up to a day: Litestream replicates the database to Tigris and a new machine restores it (docs/redundancy.md). `GET /health/deep` is live and an outside monitor (UptimeRobot, email alerts) calls it every 5 minutes, and a standby in a second region is not built. *Why one machine:* the poller, the sockets and the database are one process, and one alert per id relies on one writer.
- **A playoff series shows under "Coming up" only once both teams are known.** ESPN lists an undecided side as a placeholder (id -2, "Rays/Guardians"), which `pickUpcoming` (`server/src/series.ts`) skips, so the World Series card appears the day its second pennant is won, not 7 days ahead; showing it with a "to be decided" side and no vote buttons is not built.
- **Special Events votes are open to anyone who registers a device.** A vote is one per device id, and `/devices` registration needs no proof of a person, so someone who registers many devices can stuff a ballot. *Why it stands:* the votes are a fun poll and decide nothing (the alert toggle only tracks the team you picked, for you); *workaround:* none; a receipt or sign-in check would be the fix (docs/scores-tab.md, "Coming up"; the same ballot stays open under a series that has started).
- **The Special Events alert toggle is a temporary follow, so it shows in Tracking and counts toward the leaderboard while it lasts.** It is a row in `follows` with `event_key` and `event_until`, which is what makes every tracked-team alert work. *Why it stands:* a separate alert path would copy every alert's rules; *workaround:* none, it disappears by itself 3 hours after the series is won or the game is final, or when the cap passes (21 days for a series, 12 hours after the start of a one-off game), and a series that never reaches a finish on ESPN's board ends at the cap (docs/scores-tab.md, "The alert toggle").
- **A developer notice reaches only the devices registered when it's sent,** and only the latest one goes out live (see "Developer notices").
