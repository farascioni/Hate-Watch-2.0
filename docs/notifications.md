## Notifications (every alert type, all user-controllable)

**In short:** every alert type a user can switch is one entry in `EVENT_TYPES` (`server/src/event-types.ts`): 152 types today, 99 of them pushed by default, 37 feed only (on, in the feed, no notification) and 16 off. The table below says what each league's alerts are and how they're detected. Under the table: the defaults new installs get, what older installs kept, and every control in Settings, on a player's or team's own alert screen and on the Tracking tab. A game, news or standings alert goes through `publish()` in `server/src/fanout.ts` (the weekly recap through `publishTo`; developer notices, which are never pushed, through neither). It is stored once by its id. If it's a loss, it counts on the Successful Hate Watch tally. It reaches the feed of each device that tracks its target (or one of the team's players, with "Get their team's alerts" on) and has the alert switched on, as one alert per thing that happened. It's pushed only when push is on, the target isn't muted, it isn't quiet hours in the device's stored time zone, and the bell of the alert (or of a fact folded into it) says notify. "How a push is decided", below, goes through it step by step; [docs/duplicate-alerts.md](duplicate-alerts.md) covers how one thing happening stays one alert.

| EPL how | Soccer has no play-by-play worth reading (ESPN's core feed has every touch, 1,500 a match). The tracker reads the summary's **key events** every 2s instead: goals, own goals, penalties, cards, substitutions (`fromKeyEvents` in `detectors.ts`). They carry no score, so it's counted from the goals: each goal is the credited team's, and an own goal is credited to the team it counts for. The keeper in goal starts from the line-ups (`keepers`), then follows substitutions (a player listed as a keeper coming on) and red cards (a keeper sent off is out of goal). A penalty miss or a red card is one alert type for the player and the team, sharing a moment, so tracking both gets you one. A keeper who scores an own goal gets the own-goal alert, which counts for "concedes" too. A draw isn't a loss, so it sends no "Successful Hate Watch". Replayed against five real September 2026 matches (an own goal, a saved penalty, a second yellow, a straight red, a 5-0): every counted score matched ESPN's final. |
| Soccer fouls and penalties conceded | From the summary's **commentary**, already read every 2s (`fromCommentary`). ESPN writes each foul twice under one play id ("Foul by Kobbie Mainoo (Manchester United)." and "Josh King (Fulham) wins a free kick…"). The fouler is the one the **text** names, matched to the line-ups, because a play's participant list can be stale (one said Maxim De Cuyper for "Foul by Chema Andrés", and ESPN's stats charged Andrés); commentary spells some names its own way ("Abdul Fatawu" is "Fatawu Issahaku"), so a name that isn't exact matches the one player on that club who shares part of it. Handballs count, and so do dives ("… has gone down, but the referee deems it simulation"), as ESPN's stats do. A foul in the box adds "Penalty conceded by …": that's "Gives away a penalty" for the player and their team (one alert for someone tracking both), and it counts as the player's foul too, so it's one alert when both are in the same read (when ESPN adds the penalty a read after the foul, the penalty alerts go out then, after the foul's). Checked against ESPN's own foulsCommitted for every player in five EPL and three La Liga matches: 159 fouls, every player's count matched. |
| Soccer passes and dribbles | From ESPN's **touch-by-touch feed** (the core API: every pass, dribble and tackle, about 1,500 a match), which says what happened but not whether it worked, so each touch is judged by the next one (`soccerTouch`). **Loses the ball:** a dribble ("Take On") straight into the other team's tackle (14 of 29 take-ons in Fulham v Man United), or "Dispossessed". A tackle that missed ("Attempted tackle") means they got past. **Gives the ball away:** a pass whose next touch is the other team's: intercepted, blocked, cleared, or played straight to them. Their foul, a failed tackle or a header duel isn't. About 8-10% of passes, several a match for a busy midfielder, so it's off by default. The feed is read every 10s, a page of 100 at a time from where it stopped (the first read jumps to the newest page), and only while someone tracks a player in the match. |

| F1 how | There's no play-by-play: a race weekend is one ESPN event whose sessions (FP1…Qualifying…Race) are competitions. While a race or sprint runs, the server polls the status of **only the followed drivers** every 15s (including both cars of a followed constructor), so DNFs and double DNFs arrive live. Every 30s it also reads **every car's timing** from ESPN's core API (`readBoard` in `f1.ts`, about 45 small requests): place, gap to the leader, laps down, pit stops and status, plus the session's lap, length and flag. That fills the race's card (`withBoard`) and tells a followed driver's live alerts (`boardAlerts`, against the read before; the first is a baseline): a pit stop, a read later, with where they came out ("Charles Leclerc pitted and dropped from P3 to P5", feed only by default); lapped ("Lance Stroll was lapped by Max Verstappen", once a session, held for two reads); and 3+ places behind their grid slot ("Lando Norris is down to P9 from P4 on the grid", once a session, held for three reads with no stop of theirs, and not just a pit cycle: a car ahead counts only if it has stopped as often, `placesLost`). Not on the last lap, when a car the leader has finished ahead of looks a lap down, nor once the flag falls. A finish alert then doesn't count lapped or places lost again, though its title still says them. When a session completes, it reads the classification, and again when it changes and holds (a penalty): an alert whose facts changed is corrected in place, with no push. Each driver gets **one** alert per session with the facts merged ("Pierre Gasly finished P16 from P9 on the grid: no points, behind teammate Franco Colapinto (P13)"), counting for each matching toggle. Qualifying knockouts use classification position: the top 10 reach Q3, and the rest split evenly between Q2 and Q1 (22 cars: P11–16 / P17–22). Results are only announced for sessions that started in the last 8 hours, so a deploy never re-announces an old race. |

From ESPN's league news (`server/src/news.ts`), read every 3 minutes per league (`HW_NEWS_MS`). Only ESPN's news items (HeadlineNews) count, not the videos and columns about the same story. **Off-field trouble:** arrests, charges, DUI, lawsuits, allegations, investigations, being placed on leave ("Suns' Dillon Brooks charged with DUI from March arrest"). **Fined or suspended:** fines, suspensions, bans ("Francisco Rivero, Raudi Perez suspended for positive drug tests"). A new install (`POST /devices` in `server/src/api.ts`) stores `DEFAULT_PREFS` (`fanout.ts`): push on, sound on, quiet hours off (set to 23:00 to 08:00, America/New_York, until the device turns them on), and empty maps for every choice (`leagues`, `types`, `muted`, `targetTypes`, `pushTypes`, `targetPushTypes`, `playerTeams`). Only what a device chooses is stored; everything it hasn't touched is read from the type's default at the moment an alert is judged (`typeEnabled`: `types[id] ?? defaultOn`; `pushTypeFor`: the target's bell, then the Settings bell, then `defaultPush`, then push). So a default changed in `event-types.ts` reaches every device that never chose, at the next deploy, unless it's listed for keeping, below.

Installs from before a default change keep what they had. Each change of defaults so far has a one-time migration in `fanout.ts` that runs when the server boots, writes the old default into each existing device's settings wherever the device hadn't chosen (its own choices are spread over the old defaults, so they win), and marks itself done in `kv` so it never runs again; a device created after it gets the new defaults. A later default change needs its own list and its own key.

- `keepOldDefaults` (kv `prefs:quiet-defaults`, October 2026, the quieter defaults): existing devices keep `WERE_ON` switched on (Opponent has runners in scoring position, the NBA's and the WNBA's Misses a shot, every soccer league's Loses the ball) and `WERE_PUSHED` pushed (Gives up runs, No quality start, Loses a challenge, Strands runners in scoring position, Gets sacked, the NBA's and WNBA's Misses a free throw and Turns it over, Shoots and misses, Drops in the drivers' championship, Opponent scores, Falls behind, Drops in standings, Losing streak, A player gets injured, every soccer league's Commits a foul). Alerts added after it (Goes down in order, Goes hitless, Pits and the rest) were never anyone's, so everyone has their new defaults.
- `lossFactsFollowLoss` (kv `prefs:loss-facts`, October 2026): when a loss's facts became alerts of their own (Loses at the last second, Loses as the favorite, Gets shut out, Gets swept, Loses to a worse team, Falls below .500: `LOSS_FACT_TYPES`), a device that had "Loses a game" off, in Settings or for one team, got all six off too, the same way, so a loss it had silenced doesn't come back as "lost as 64% favorites".
- `keepFlagsPushed` (kv `prefs:nfl-flags-feed`, October 11, 2026): an NFL player's flag (`nfl.penalty`, "Commits a penalty") became feed only for new installs after the Jaguars' trackers got 14 pushes for 19 flags in London; existing devices keep it pushed (`FLAGS_WERE_PUSHED`).

`GET` and `PUT /me/prefs` (`api.ts`) answer with `prefsDto(prefs)`: every feed-only-by-default alert's bell filled in as off, so app builds that read a missing bell as "push" draw it right. What's stored stays only what the device chose (`test/quiet-defaults.test.ts` checks both). Settings let users control:

- push on/off: Settings → Delivery → "Push notifications", stored as `pushEnabled`. Off, nothing is pushed to the device, while every alert still lands in the feed and reaches the open app over the WebSocket. The switch shows on only when `pushEnabled` is on and the phone's permission is granted ("Blocked in system settings — the feed still updates live." when it isn't). Turning it on without permission asks for it first (`enablePush` in `app/src/lib/store.tsx`). A device with no push token (permission never given, or Expo reported the token `DeviceNotRegistered`, which `server/src/push.ts` clears) gets no pushes either way: `publish()` pushes only to a device with a token.
- sound: "Sound", stored as `sound`. On, each push carries the default sound; off, it's sent with no sound (`sound: null`) and still shows on the lock screen. It covers the weekly recap's push too (`publishTo`).
- quiet hours in their own timezone (alerts still reach the feed): "Quiet hours", with From and Until steppers in whole hours (12 AM to 11 PM), stored as `quietHours: { enabled, start, end, tz }`. Flipping the switch (on or off) stores the phone's time zone (`Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone`); changing the hours doesn't, so after changing time zones, switch it off and on to pick up the new one. `inQuietHours` (`fanout.ts`) formats the time when the alert is published as "HH:MM" in that zone and compares: the start is inside, the end isn't, and a start later than the end runs over midnight. With 23:00 to 08:00 in America/Chicago, a strikeout at 11:30 PM Chicago time and one at 7:59 AM are quiet, and one at 8:00 AM pushes. With 13:00 to 15:00, only the afternoon is quiet. A start equal to the end is never quiet. A quiet alert is stored as not pushed and is never pushed later: when quiet hours end, nothing catches up. The open app still buzzes for a live alert unless its target is muted (`store.tsx`). The weekly recap's push follows quiet hours too (it still lands in the feed), and the recap goes out by this time zone ("US Eastern until you turn them on").
- each league on/off: no longer a switch of its own. - **a 🔔 on every alert type: notify me, or feed only.** Off keeps that alert in the feed without a push ("Feed only: no notification" under it). - **How a push is decided** (`publish()` in `fanout.ts`), for each alert in each publish, in this order:
   1. **Who it can reach** (`recipients`): every device tracking its target, and for a team's alert, every device tracking one of that team's players with "Get their team's alerts" on, once per device. With nobody, the alert isn't stored at all, so its id stays free for when someone follows.
   2. **Stored once:** inserted into `events` with `ON CONFLICT(id) DO NOTHING`. An id already there (a re-poll, a restart, the same play from two feeds) stops here: no feed row, no live frame, no push.
   3. **Counted:** a Successful Hate Watch is recorded for every one of those devices before any setting is looked at (`recordHateWatch`), so switching "Loses a game" off doesn't stop the counter.
   4. **Delivered at all** (`shouldDeliver`): the alert's type, or any of its `aliases` (other switches that cover it), must be on for that target (`typeEnabledFor`: the target's own switch if it has one, else the league not switched off and the Settings switch, else `defaultOn`). Then an alert marked `unless: X` is dropped for a device that wants X for that target, because X says the same thing more specifically (stranded runners, `unless` the NOBLETIGER). Muting and quiet hours don't come into it: they only stop the push.
   5. **One alert per thing that happened:** a `lateOn` alert becomes a line on the device's latest alert about it when there is one (a standings drop after a loss); of a moment's `firstFollowed` alerts the device gets the one about what it followed first; of a `moment`'s alerts it gets the first it wants, and the later ones become lines on it (their `fold`), at most one per `alt` group, or are dropped. A fact that comes in a later publish, within 6 hours, becomes a line on the alert already sent, with no push. All of this is in [docs/duplicate-alerts.md](duplicate-alerts.md), Layer 4.
   6. **Pushed or not** (`willPush`): the device has a push token; `pushAllowed` says yes (push on, this target not muted, not quiet hours now); if the alert reached it through a tracked player ("Get their team's alerts"), that player isn't muted; and `pushWanted` says yes for the alert or for any fact folded into it. `pushWanted` takes the first of the alert's type and its aliases that's switched on for the target and asks its bell (`pushTypeFor`: the target's bell, then the Settings bell, then `defaultPush`, then push).
   7. **Sent:** the device's feed row (`pushed` 1 or 0, the folded lines in `extra`), an `event` frame to each socket the device has open (the lines in front of the body), and if it's pushed, a notification titled with the alert's emoji and title, the lines and body as its text, the device's sound choice, and a `threadId` that stacks a team's alerts and its players' together on iOS (`pushThread`). An F1 device's alerts from one session in one publish are one notification ("Singapore GP · Sprint: 7 alerts", `bundled`).

The choice stays while the app is open.
- **How many people hate them:** every player and team row says how many people (devices) track them: the Tracking tab, Search results and its team list, and team rosters ("👁 12 haters", "1 hater (just you)", "No haters yet"). A player or team's own page shows it under their name. `GET /search`, `GET /teams` and `GET /targets/:key` (with its roster) carry `haters` (`withHaters` in `leaderboard.ts`). The app keeps the freshest count it has seen for each one (`noteTrackers` in the store), so tracking someone from Search bumps their number right away. A player counts on their own, not toward their team. It's a count over `follows` (indexed by target), so there's nothing extra to store. `GET /me/follows` returns `trackers` on each follow. Following or unfollowing returns the new count, so the number updates at once, and the tab reloads the counts each time it opens. Deleting a device's data removes it from every count.
- **A tracked player's team ("Their team" on the player's ⚙️ screen):** two switches. **Show their team's games** (on by default) puts the team's games in My games and Up next on the Scores tab, as tracking a player always did; off, they're left out unless you track the team or another of its players with it on (`deviceTeams` in `scores.ts`; F1 drivers don't have it, since F1 sessions show for any F1 follow). **Get their team's alerts** (off by default) sends the team's own alerts too, as if you tracked the team, by your Team alerts settings: game starts, losses, opponent scores and the rest (`recipients` in `fanout.ts`). It's one copy per device, tracking the team too or not (a device that tracks the team gets it as the team's follower), and the player's mute keeps them feed only. With it on for Aaron Judge, a Yankees loss is a Successful Hate Watch you'd have had anyway through "Their team loses", and both share the loss's moment, so it's still one alert. Stored as `playerTeams` (playerKey → `{ scores, alerts }`; a `null` in a PUT goes back to the default; only player keys and those two fields are kept, `mergePlayerTeams`) in prefs (`test/player-team.test.ts`). Tracking the team itself gets both, so the switches are greyed out then.
- **per-target ⭐ on the Tracking tab (Hate Watch Plus): your most hated.** Marks a player or team; their alerts in the Feed get a gold border. Kept on the device only, no effect on which alerts or pushes arrive; docs/deploy.md has the details.
- **Tracking tab card layout (the "Layout" button in the top right of the page, the header).** Five looks for the cards, picked in a sheet and kept on the device only (`hw.trackingLayout`, `app/src/lib/trackingLayout.ts`): Classic (the banner card, the default), Compact (a colour bar, the game on the right), Banner game (the banner carries LIVE / NEXT / FINAL and the game), Tiles (two cards per row) and Scoreboard (a strip of record, form and game). The denser four (`TrackingCard.tsx`) show the team's record and streak as pills, and a college team's conference standing as a third (the Scoreboard shows it in the line under the strip when there is no game) (a player's card shows their team's) and one game line: the live game, else the nearest one to come, else the latest final, else the "Up next" game (`gameInfo` in `app/src/lib/trackingInfo.ts`, from the store's `games` and `upNext`; nothing for F1 and UFC). They show the buttons as icons only, and the close icon asks before it unfollows. Record and streak come from `GET /teams?by=division`, so they show once the server is deployed and are simply left out before.
- **per-target ⚙️ on the Tracking tab: choose exactly which alerts you get for one player or team.** These choices beat every global alert setting for that target, including the type and league switches (`typeEnabledFor`: the target's own choice, if there is one, decides alone). For example, interceptions stay on globally but are off for Daniel Jones alone, or a league is off globally but one player's alert is explicitly on. A row you haven't touched says "Global setting (on)", "(on, feed only)" or "(off)"; one you have says "Custom for …" with "Use global setting", which clears both its switch and its bell; "Reset all N to global settings" clears every one. The gear turns red when a target has any custom switch or bell. The screen lists only the alerts that can fire for that kind of target in that league (a type whose `scope` is that kind, or whose `alsoScope` is, for one sent to both: the NFL's "Gives up a safety" is on a team's screen and an offensive player's), at the player's positions. Stored as `targetTypes` (targetKey → typeId → on/off) in prefs; a `null` in a PUT resets one back to global, and unknown types or malformed keys are ignored (`mergeTargetTypes`). The "one alert, not two" rules (NOBLETIGER vs stranded, lead changes) use the same per-target answer.
- reset to defaults: Settings → Data → "Reset alerts to defaults" writes every alert type's switch as its `defaultOn`, every bell as its `defaultPush`, and every league as on. It leaves the per-target choices, mutes, quiet hours, sound and the push switch as they are, and asks nothing first. Because it writes values rather than clearing them, a reset device keeps today's defaults even if a later deploy changes them.
- clear feed: Settings → Data → "Clear feed" (after "Clear feed?"/"This removes every item from your feed." on a phone) deletes the device's feed rows (`DELETE /me/feed`). The alerts themselves stay stored, so they're never sent again, the Successful Hate Watch counter keeps its count, and a line that comes later for a cleared alert is dropped rather than sent as a new alert (`addLateLine`; `test/bundling.test.ts`).

A homer counts as "gives up runs" too, so turning off "gives up a HR" alone won't hide the runs: the homer still arrives, as a run (see "How a push is decided"). Every homer is its own "Gives up a home run" alert, and no separate runs alert goes out for that play (`detectors.ts`); its title says how far it went when ESPN does ("Sean Burke gave up a 348-foot 2-run homer"), a grand slam says so, and one hit right after another off the same pitcher in a half-inning says so: "Gerrit Cole gave up back-to-back homers (380 feet)", the third "back-to-back-to-back".
