NBA rival alerts

Basketball moves fast and an 82-game season is long, so nobody can watch every possession of the team they dislike. Hate Watch watches for you and sends the moments you would have wanted to see: the block, the airball, the technical, the lead that disappears.

What you get alerts for

NBA alerts are mostly about one player at a time. You can get an alert when your target:

  • Gets blocked;
  • Misses a free throw, or misses a shot (with the distance, and a note when it was late and close);
  • Turns it over;
  • Has a brick night, which is a bad shooting game, or is scoreless at the half;
  • Gets a technical foul or is ejected, or fouls out;
  • Commits a foul, if you want every one.

Two alerts are about the team instead: the opponent's star going off, which tells you the other side's best player is having a big night against your target's team, and the opponent going on a run, which tells you your target's team is getting outscored in a stretch. Alerts like a missed shot or a foul are numerous, so some are off or feed-only until you ask for them.

Which ones buzz your phone

Push notifications by default include a block, a technical foul or ejection, a foul-out and the opponent's star going off. Missed free throws, turnovers, brick nights, scoreless halves and opponent runs go to the feed without a buzz, and missed shots and ordinary fouls are off. Change any of them in Settings, for the whole league or for one player.

When the game ends

When a team you track loses, you get the loss alert plus lines for how: they lost on a go-ahead score in the last 10 seconds or in overtime, they blew a lead of 15 points or more, they lost as a heavy favorite, or they lost to a team with a much worse record. If a player scored 40 or more against them, the alert says so. The standings count too: you hear when a team falls below .500, drops in the standings, or is eliminated.

Picking a target

Lakers and Celtics, Knicks and Heat, Warriors and whoever beat them last: the NBA has no shortage of teams to root against. Tracking a single player is often more fun, because an individual's bad night is easy to enjoy and the per-player settings let you pick exactly which of their failures you want to hear about. Search for a team or a player and tap Track. The WNBA gets the same set of alerts.
